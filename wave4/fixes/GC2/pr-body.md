The iterator of a Range or NumericRange that spans more than half its element type reports a wrong `knownSize`, and consumers that size themselves from it drop elements. `(Int.MinValue to Int.MaxValue by (1 << 30)).iterator.toList` is empty instead of `List(-2147483648, -1073741824, 0, 1073741824)`; `(Int.MinValue to Int.MaxValue by Int.MaxValue).iterator.toVector` has one element of three; `NumericRange.inclusive[Byte](-128, 57, 5).iterator.knownSize` is -13, which the `IterableOnce` contract does not allow. `(Long.MinValue to Long.MaxValue by (1L << 62)).iterator` is empty under `toVector`, `toArray` and `take(2).toList`. Methods on the range itself (`toList`, `map`, `length`) are right; only paths through `.iterator` are affected, such as a for-yield over `r.iterator` or `Vector(1) ++ r.iterator`.

`RangeIterator.knownSize` (`Range.scala`) and `NumericRangeIterator.knownSize` (`NumericRange.scala`) compute `(lastElement - next) / step + 1` in the element type. When the range is wider than the type's positive half, the subtraction wraps. Both have been wrong since `knownSize` was added to these iterators in 2.13.0; 2.12 iterators had no `knownSize`.

`RangeIterator` now counts in Long and returns -1 when more than `Int.MaxValue` elements remain (only `Int.MinValue to Int.MaxValue` by 1 or -1). `NumericRangeIterator` works for any `Integral`, so it detects the wrap from the sign of the difference against the sign of the step, and returns -1 then or when the count does not fit in an Int. A wide Long range therefore reports -1 rather than its exact size, which is allowed and makes consumers grow instead of truncate; an exact count would need a counter field in a serializable iterator.
